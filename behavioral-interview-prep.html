Q01 Tell me about a time when you had to influence a decision without having direct authority over the stakeholders. ▾

Result
All teams voluntarily adopted the platform within 6 months. Consistent observability across Azure and GCP, reducing MTTR by 40% and eliminating tool sprawl costing $200K+ annually.

Q04 Describe a time when you had to solve a complex performance problem under pressure. ▾
Situation
At American Express, high-volume APIs powering AmericanExpress.com started experiencing degraded response times during peak hours. 48 hours before a major marketing campaign that would increase traffic by 40%.
Task
Identify root cause and implement fixes before the campaign launched without a risky deployment during peak hours.
Action
  • Pulled Real User Metrics (RUM) and distributed tracing — identified database connection pool exhaustion
  • Assembled a war room with database, application, and infrastructure teams
  • Rather than increasing pool size (masking the issue), analyzed query patterns and found N+1 queries in a recently deployed feature
  • Implemented query optimization and introduced caching for hot-path data
  • Created a performance testing framework to simulate campaign load and validate fixes
  • Established canary deployment to roll out fixes incrementally with automated rollback
Result
Response times improved by 60% before campaign. Handled 50% more traffic than projected with better performance than baseline. Performance testing framework became standard for all future releases.

Handling Failure & Learning

Q9 – Q10
Q09 Tell me about a time when something you architected didn't work as planned. What did you learn? ▾
Situation
At American Express, I designed a caching strategy for high-volume APIs that I believed would significantly improve performance. After deployment, we saw minimal improvement and actually increased infrastructure costs.
Task
Understand why my design didn't deliver expected results and correct course quickly.
Action
  • Immediately pulled back from broader rollout and analyzed production metrics
  • Discovered cache hit rate assumptions were wrong — traffic patterns had more unique requests than modeled from test data
  • Realized I'd optimized for the wrong metric (response time) when the actual bottleneck was database connection management
  • Conducted a blameless post-mortem with the team to understand what we missed
  • Pivoted to connection pooling optimization and query pattern analysis
  • Updated architecture review checklist to validate caching assumptions with production-realistic data patterns
Result
Revised approach improved performance by 60% at lower cost. Learned to validate architectural assumptions with production-realistic data. Now always include observability instrumentation in designs to validate and course-correct quickly.
